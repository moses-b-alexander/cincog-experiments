Reading a score

Rank CDF at k
Fraction of targets found in the first k positions. At k = 1 this is exact recovery.
Top-k mass
Sum of the k largest probabilities, averaged across responses. Its complement is the omitted mass.
Target surprisal
−log₂ of the probability assigned to the true word. A uniform guess from 64 words costs six bits.
Multiclass Brier score
Sum of squared errors over the full menu against a one-hot target. Zero is perfect.
Total variation
Half the summed absolute difference between two distributions. In the Kev example, 4.475% of the mass moves.
Jensen–Shannon divergence
Mean KL divergence to the midpoint distribution. The base and bsln replies differ by 0.002242 bits. Both hide the active rule. Comparison JSON

Menu and context limits

  • The native endpoint returned all 64 options on the qualified checkpoints. Text length, tokenization and the loaded context limit which menus fit. Decision API
  • The earlier study allocated 73,728 tokens for Kev 0.8B, 4,096 for D3 2B/4B, and 2,048 for Kev 4B/9B. These are run-specific allocations. Context settings
  • The tested chat endpoint returned at most 20 vocabulary alternatives. Conventional LM comparisons require a distinct single-token code per action and native scores for every legal code.
Why use native probabilities?

Asking a model to write percentages changes the response task. We read native choice weights or token probabilities at the scored action. Distributional alignment · First-token rankings and final answers
